Step by step

How to translate subtitles on Android TV.

  1. Install ARVIO and choose your subtitle language

    Get the free app from Google Play or the official GitHub release. Set your preferred subtitle language in settings; translation needs a target language.

  2. Configure the AI Subtitles controls

    In Settings, find AI Subtitles, available within General on TV layouts. Enable AI Subtitle Translation and select Groq or Google Gemini under AI Model. Labels and grouping can vary with layout and app version.

  3. Add the matching provider key

    Enter your own API Key for the selected provider. Android settings also offer Scan QR Code to Set Key so you can enter the credential through your phone instead of a TV remote. Keep the key private and check the provider's account requirements.

  4. Play a title with readable subtitles

    Open the player's subtitle menu and choose its available AI translation option. This path needs an eligible text subtitle source. Try an embedded or external text track rather than assuming that a file without subtitles will work.

  5. Check meaning and timing

    Compare translated text with the original. If it is late or inaccurate, switch back to a regular track or choose another source. Auto-Select AI Translation is optional and still requires suitable source text.

Choose the right tool

Subtitle translation, search and styling are different.

Translated cues follow the source track's timing. Translating poorly synchronized subtitles does not automatically correct their timestamps.

Find an existing subtitle first

Check embedded tracks or search compatible subtitle sources. A good original track in your preferred language can avoid AI costs and translation errors entirely.

Translate readable text

AI subtitle translation works on text. Image-based PGS tracks do not provide readable text for this path. If no eligible source appears, try a different text track or an authorized external subtitle source.

Adjust timing separately

Use subtitle offset controls when text appears early or late. Translation does not reconstruct missing lines or repair the source's timing automatically.

Make subtitles readable from the sofa

Adjust supported subtitle size, colour, style and position. Supported layouts can position text relative to the full display, including available black-bar space, rather than always covering the picture.

A separate player implementation

Translate subtitles in ARVIO Web.

Open Settings → AI Subtitles, enable AI subtitle enhancement, select Groq or Gemini under AI model and enter AI API key. In the player, open Subtitles and choose AI translation when an eligible English subtitle source is available. Set your preferred subtitle language before testing.

The browser implementation translates existing subtitle cues; it is not an arbitrary audio-to-text transcription service. Embedded subtitle access and playback capabilities depend on the browser and source. Do not assume Android and browser players offer identical controls.

Free self-hosted browser profiles and settings stay local to that browser. Optional managed hosting and supported ARVIO Cloud preferences are separate; see the ARVIO Web setup guide.

Check AI costs and privacy before enabling translation.

ARVIO does not include an AI subscription or guarantee free unlimited translation. Your provider may require billing, impose quotas or change model availability. Check Groq's rate limits or Gemini API billing documentation rather than relying on a fixed per-movie estimate.

Subtitle text is sent to the selected provider. Review its privacy and retention terms before using sensitive content. AI translations can lose context, mistranslate names or contain errors. They are not authoritative transcripts or guaranteed accessibility captions.

Common questions and troubleshooting

AI subtitle translation FAQ.

Does ARVIO include an AI subscription?

No. AI subtitle translation is optional and uses your own Groq or Gemini API key. Provider billing, quotas and model availability apply separately from ARVIO.

Why is AI subtitle translation unavailable?

Check that AI translation is enabled, the selected provider matches your API key and a target subtitle language is set. The text-translation path also needs an eligible readable subtitle source; image-based PGS tracks do not supply text.

Why did translation stop or show the original text?

Check the API key, account quota, billing requirements, network connection and provider availability. Translation can pause or fall back when requests fail or are rate-limited. Switch to an ordinary subtitle track while resolving the provider issue.

Does translating subtitles fix bad timing?

No. Translation follows the source subtitle cues. Use subtitle offset controls or choose a better synchronized source if the original track appears early or late.

Can subtitles be moved onto black bars?

ARVIO offers subtitle position and styling controls, and supported player layouts can position text relative to the full display. The result depends on the player, video aspect ratio and available display space.

Are AI-translated subtitles always accurate?

No. Translations may contain errors, omit context or mishandle names and dialogue. Review the output and keep a regular subtitle track available as a fallback.
